ModelThe model is decided before opening Power BI.

It’s the step nobody sees, and everything people do see depends on it. Here I decide what each row represents, what shape the tables take and how each number is computed.

00What modelling is

Modelling is arranging the dataso every question has one single answer.

The problem

✗They ask if we hit the budget. The table says 20%.

✓We sold $8,000 of $10,000: that’s 80%. The table lies.

↓ First: what is each row?

Grain

✗The monthly budget repeats on every row.

✓Each row is one receipt line. The budget leaves the table.

↓ What’s left splits in two.

Fact table

✗Numbers and descriptions, all together.

✓What happened stays here: quantity and amount.

↓ Each sale’s context is missing.

Dimension tables

✗“Downtown” and “Coffee” repeat across thousands of rows.

✓Each value, once: when, where, what and who.

↓ Now they need joining.

Keys and relationships

✗The tables don’t talk, and the budget is still $40,000.

✓A key joins them. The budget arrives once: $10,000.

↓ Only the calculation is left.

Measures

✗The 20% was stored in the table.

✓The measure computes with what you filter: $8,000 ÷ $10,000 = 80%.

✓ Done: one single answer.

    00The schema

    I use star schema.Everything else, only with a reason.

    I know them all. I use very few, and it’s good to know why.

    ✓What I use
    Exceptions, only with a reason
    For other kinds of systems: I don’t use them
    How common in BI
    ✓I use itBy default

    Star schema

    What it is

    One fact table in the center, with its dimension tables around it.

    Used in

    BI and Power BI. Recommended by Microsoft and Kimball.

    Not used in

    Systems that record operations.

    How common in BI
    ✓I use itWith several processes

    Galaxy schema

    What it is

    Several fact tables sharing dimensions.

    Used in

    Comparing processes: sales against stock or budget.

    Not used in

    Projects with a single process.

    How common in BI
    !ExceptionOnly with a reason

    Snowflake schema

    What it is

    A star schema with dimensions split into more tables.

    Used in

    Large warehouses, or a dimension shared at another grain.

    Not used in

    Power BI, if it’s just to “tidy up”: more joins and slower.

    How common in BI
    !ExceptionExports and one-off analysis

    OBT · One Big Table

    What it is

    Everything in one wide table, no joins.

    Used in

    Direct SQL, data science and exports. Runs well in BigQuery.

    Not used in

    The final delivery in Power BI.

    How common in BI
    ×I don’t use itI can read it

    3NF

    What it is

    Normalized tables, no repeated data.

    Used in

    Transactional systems (OLTP): tills, ERP, CRM.

    Not used in

    Reporting directly on it.

    How common in BI
    ×I don’t use itI know it

    Data Vault

    What it is

    Hubs, links and satellites to keep the full history.

    Used in

    Large companies: many sources, audit and a data engineering team.

    Not used in

    Small companies or few sources.

    How common in BI
    ×I don’t use itI know it

    Graph

    What it is

    Nodes and edges, for multi-hop questions.

    Used in

    Fraud, networks and recommendations.

    Not used in

    What two joins can solve.
